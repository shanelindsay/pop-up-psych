# Image Prompts

This file collects reusable image-generation prompts for Pop-Up Psychology experiment station posters.

The current direction is an A4 portrait poster that sits beside a public psychology experiment. The poster should help a student experimenter run the activity and give them a short debrief to deliver afterwards.

## House Style

Use this block at the start of each poster prompt.

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Project: Pop-Up Psychology
Format: A4 portrait experiment station poster.
Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help a student experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Header:
Empty thin header band across the top 10-15% of the poster.

Visual identity: Perception Studio.
Use soft ivory paper, graphite typography, electric teal, persimmon, aubergine, and pale mint accents. The design should feel tactile, editorial, curious, mature, and suitable for psychology outreach.

Typography:
Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. The typography should feel intentionally designed rather than generic system sans.
```

## Semantic Priming

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Project: Pop-Up Psychology
Format: A4 portrait experiment station poster.
Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help a student experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Header:
Empty thin header band across the top 10-15% of the poster.

Visual identity: Perception Studio.
Use soft ivory paper, graphite typography, electric teal, persimmon, aubergine, and pale mint accents. The design should feel tactile, editorial, curious, mature, and suitable for psychology outreach.

Typography:
Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. The typography should feel intentionally designed rather than generic system sans.

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
SEMANTIC PRIMING

Main question:
Can one word change your next answer?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
Ask quickly:
1 What colour is snow?
2 How do you spell SILK?
3 What do cows drink?

Response section title:
After they answer

Response section text:
Record the first answer to question 3.
Many people say MILK.
The correct answer is WATER.

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- The first questions activate WHITE and SILK.
- That makes MILK more available in memory.
- Under time pressure, participants may answer with the primed word before checking the final question.
- This is semantic priming: one concept activates related concepts in memory.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but together take no more than 15% of the poster height below the empty header.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact spoken script. This is the most important content on the poster and should be the largest content block.

Response section:
Place the After they answer section directly beside or beneath the script so the experimenter knows what to record and reveal.

Visual treatment:
Use a subtle semantic word-field around the experiment content, with words like WHITE, SILK, MILK, WATER, SNOW, COWS, and DRINK appearing as quiet editorial background fragments. The word-field should add visual energy without competing with the experiment script.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the experiment script.

EXCLUSIONS
No QR code.
No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No cartoon brain icon.
No decorative icons in content blocks.
No duplicate mini panels.
No explanatory diagram.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No text, marks, icons, boxes, or placeholder elements in the empty header band.
```

## Hollow Face Illusion

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
HOLLOW FACE ILLUSION

Main question:
Why does a hollow face look solid?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
1 Show the white Einstein mask.
2 Ask: is the face pointing in or out?
3 Slowly rotate the mask.
4 Ask when the illusion flips.
5 Let them move side to side.

Observation section title:
WHAT TO WATCH FOR

Observation section text:
Record when the face flips from protruding out to hollow.
The reversed mask can still look like a face protruding towards you.

Image labels:
Mask protrudes towards you
Mask protrudes away from you
Board shows viewing angle
Face expectation wins

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- The mask is hollow, but it can still look like it is protruding towards you.
- Your visual system expects faces to be convex.
- That expectation can outweigh depth cues from the backing board, shading, and motion.
- This is top-down processing: prior knowledge shapes what you perceive.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but together take no more than 15% of the poster height below the empty header.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact procedure. This is the most important content block.

Observation section:
Place the WHAT TO WATCH FOR text directly beside or beneath the experiment panel.

Main visual:
Use the attached reference image to make the object accurate. Show a glossy white plastic Einstein face mask mounted on a flat white rectangular board. Include two comparison views of the same object: one labelled Mask protrudes towards you and one labelled Mask protrudes away from you. Use the rectangular backing board and viewing angle to make the illusion understandable. The object should look like the real white plastic Einstein mask on a board, not a generic theatre mask or freestanding bust.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the experiment procedure.

EXCLUSIONS
No QR code.
No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No cartoon brain icon.
No atom icon.
No decorative science icons.
No duplicate mini panels.
No second diagram.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No text, marks, icons, boxes, or placeholder elements in the empty header band.

## Upside-Down Goggles

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Project: Pop-Up Psychology
Format: A4 portrait experiment station poster.
Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help a student experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Header:
Empty thin header band across the top 10-15% of the poster.

Visual identity: Perception Studio.
Use soft ivory paper, graphite typography, electric teal, persimmon, aubergine, and pale mint accents. The design should feel tactile, editorial, curious, mature, and suitable for psychology outreach.

Typography:
Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. The typography should feel intentionally designed rather than generic system sans.

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
UPSIDE-DOWN GOGGLES

Main question:
Can your brain adapt to an upside-down world?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
1 Seat participant
2 Hold target still
3 Ask them to touch it
4 Repeat 3 more times
5 Ask: did it improve?

Observation section title:
WHAT TO WATCH FOR

Observation section text:
First reach often misses.
Later reaches usually improve.

Image labels:
First reach misses
Later reach improves
Through the goggles

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- This is sensorimotor adaptation.
- The goggles distort visual feedback.
- Intended movements produce unexpected outcomes.
- The nervous system uses prediction errors to update the visuomotor mapping between seeing and reaching.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but should not dominate the whole poster.

Main visual:
Large central tabletop psychology experiment scene. A student experimenter holds a pen-tip target at a table while a seated participant wearing upside-down prism goggles reaches toward it. Show one missed first reach and one improved later reach using clean motion paths. Include one small inset labelled Through the goggles showing a vertically reversed view.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact procedural steps. This is the most important text block on the poster.

Observation section:
Place WHAT TO WATCH FOR directly beside or beneath the experiment steps so the student experimenter knows what to notice during repeated reaches.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the experiment steps.

EXCLUSIONS
No QR code.
No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No cartoon brain icon.
No decorative icons in content blocks.
No duplicate mini panels.
No explanatory diagram.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No text, marks, icons, boxes, or placeholder elements in the empty header band.
```
