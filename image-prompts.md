# Image Prompts

This file collects reusable image-generation prompts for Pop-Up Psychology experiment station posters.

The current direction is an A4 portrait poster that sits beside a public psychology experiment. The poster should help a student experimenter run the activity and give them a short debrief to deliver afterwards.

## House Style

Use this block at the start of each poster prompt.

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Project: Pop-Up Psychology
Format: A4 portrait experiment station poster.
Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help an experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Header:
Empty thin header band across the top 10-15% of the poster.

Visual identity: Perception Studio.
Use soft ivory paper, graphite typography, electric teal, persimmon, aubergine, and pale mint accents. The design should feel tactile, editorial, curious, mature, and suitable for psychology outreach.

Typography:
Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. The typography should feel intentionally designed rather than generic system sans.
```

## Reaction Catch Challenge

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Format: A4 portrait experiment station poster.
Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help a student experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Header:
Empty thin header band across the top 10-15% of the poster.

Visual identity: Perception Studio.
Use soft ivory paper, graphite typography, electric teal, persimmon, aubergine, and pale mint accents, with bright yellow and royal blue accents from the reaction-catching toy. The design should feel tactile, editorial, curious, mature, and suitable for psychology outreach.

Typography:
Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. Section headings should be clean typographic tabs or simple text headings.

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
REACTION CATCH CHALLENGE

Main question:
Can attention change reaction time?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
1 Ask the participant to catch as many sticks as possible.
2 Score how many they catch out of 6.
3 Ask them to count backwards by 3s while catching.
4 Score again out of 6.
5 Compare the two scores.

Observation section title:
WHAT TO WATCH FOR

Observation section text:
Does distraction reduce the score?
Does the participant change strategy?
Are some participants affected more than others?

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- Reaction time is not just fast hands.
- The brain must detect the drop, choose a response, and move.
- Counting uses working memory and attention.
- When attention is split, catching can get harder.
- Psychologists compare conditions to explain behaviour.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but should not dominate the whole poster.

Main visual:
Show the falling-stick reaction-catching toy clearly and large. The toy has a bright glossy yellow horizontal top bar shaped like a rounded plastic tube. Six evenly spaced vertical royal-blue foam sticks hang from yellow connector sockets. Each blue cylindrical stick has a yellow plastic cap at the bottom. Thin cords suspend the top bar. The sticks are ready to drop. Show only the participant's hands below the sticks, palms ready to catch, cropped at the wrists. Keep the toy as the central focus. Use clean product-style lighting on a plain light background. The object should immediately read as a falling-stick reaction challenge used in a psychology experiment.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact procedural steps. This is the most important text block on the poster.

Observation section:
Place WHAT TO WATCH FOR directly beside or beneath the experiment steps so the experimenter knows what to notice.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the experiment steps.

EXCLUSIONS
No project name.
No brand name.
No QR code.
No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No footer text.
No logos.
No watermark.
No people faces.
No cartoon brain icon.
No decorative icons in content blocks.
No duplicate mini panels.
No explanatory diagram.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No text, marks, icons, boxes, or placeholder elements in the empty header band.
No extra text beyond the VISIBLE TEXT block.
```

## Corsi Block-Tapping Task

```text
HOUSE STYLE

Audience: A-level psychology students.
Purpose: Help a student experimenter run a public psychology experiment and support a short debrief.

Use a contemporary museum typography system: a tall condensed grotesk display face for the activity title, an elegant humanist sans for the question, and a refined readable sans for instructions and debrief. Section headings should be clean typographic tabs or simple text headings.

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
CORSI BLOCK-TAPPING TASK

Main question:
What is the limit of spatial working memory?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
1 Tap each block about once per second.
2 Ask the participant to copy the sequence.
3 Start with the 2-block sequence.
4 Move to the next length after a correct response.
5 Stop after two failed attempts at the same length.

Sequence panel title:
SEQUENCES

Sequence panel text:
2: 2-8
3: 9-6-5
4: 7-2-4-5
5: 1-9-3-6-7
6: 3-4-8-7-2-6
7: 9-3-5-7-4-1-8
8: 3-8-7-5-1-2-6-9
9: 7-4-2-8-1-9-6-3-5
10: 1-8-5-7-3-9-6-4-2-8

Score section title:
RECORD THE SPAN

Score section text:
Corsi span = longest sequence length copied correctly.
Most adults score around 5-6.

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- Corsi span estimates visuo-spatial working-memory capacity.
- Longer spans require maintaining serial order, not just recognising locations.
- Errors often preserve some blocks but lose order or later positions.
- Performance drops as sequence length exceeds available working-memory resources.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but should not dominate the whole poster.

Main visual:
Show a tabletop Corsi block board with nine blocks in an irregular spatial arrangement. The participant-facing sides of the blocks are plain and unnumbered. Small numbers are visible only on the experimenter-facing side or as a separate experimenter map on the poster. The participant watches the experimenter tap locations but cannot see block numbers. The visual should support the idea of serial spatial recall without adding a separate explanatory diagram.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact procedure. This is the most important text block on the poster.

Sequence section:
Include the SEQUENCES panel clearly enough for the student experimenter to run the task from the poster. Keep the sequence list compact but readable.

Score section:
Place RECORD THE SPAN near the sequence list so the student experimenter knows what outcome to record.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the procedure and sequence list.

EXCLUSIONS
No QR code.
No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No cartoon brain icon.
No decorative icons in content blocks.
No duplicate mini panels.
No explanatory diagram.
No brush-stroke banners.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No participant-visible block numbers.
No regular 3x3 keypad layout.
No branded clothing or visible logos.
No text, marks, icons, boxes, or placeholder elements in the empty header band.
```

## Rubber Hand Illusion

```text

VISIBLE TEXT
Only this text should appear on the poster.

Activity title:
RUBBER HAND ILLUSION

Main question:
Can a fake hand feel like yours?

Primary panel title:
RUN THE EXPERIMENT

Primary panel text:
1 Seat participant at the table.
2 Hide their real hand behind a screen.
3 Place the rubber hand in view.
4 Stroke both hands together for 60 seconds.
5 Ask where they feel the touch.

Observation section title:
WHAT TO WATCH FOR

Observation section text:
The participant may feel touch on the rubber hand.
Some people feel that the rubber hand belongs to them.
A sudden threat to the rubber hand may make them flinch.

Debrief title:
DEBRIEF

Debrief text:
- Vision and touch are synchronised.
- The brain uses this multisensory evidence to judge body ownership.
- When the seen hand and felt touch match, the rubber hand can be experienced as part of the body.
- The illusion shows that the sense of my body is actively constructed.

COMPOSITION
Top section:
Activity title and main question should be visually strong but should not dominate the whole poster.

Main visual:
Use a photorealistic classroom demonstration image viewed from the experimenter's side of a black tabletop, looking toward the seated participant. The participant faces the camera and looks down naturally, with no sunglasses, headphones, goggles, or blindfold. Their normal clothing should be visible at the chest and shoulders. A small loose black drape sits locally around the forearms and hand entry points only; it hides the origins of the arms and the rubber-hand wrist but does not cover the whole torso or table.

The setup must show exactly three participant hands/arms on the table. On the viewer's left is the participant's ordinary visible real left hand, resting and not brushed. In the middle, directly in front of the participant, is the lifelike rubber right hand, palm-down, fingers pointing toward the viewer/experimenter, with its wrist tucked just under the black drape. On the viewer's right, behind the divider, is the participant's real right hand, palm-down, fingers pointing toward the viewer/experimenter. The middle rubber hand and the divider-side real hand must be the same hand orientation, like a right hand and a duplicate right rubber hand placed side by side. Their thumbs must be on the same side relative to their fingers; do not mirror one into a left hand.

A solid vertical opaque divider screen stands lengthwise exactly between the middle rubber right hand and the viewer-right real right hand. The divider runs from the participant toward the camera, parallel with the two matching right hands. It blocks the participant's view of the viewer-right real hand but does not cover or intersect any hand, wrist, fingers, or forearm. Two experimenter hands enter from the foreground with small paintbrushes: one brush strokes the middle rubber right hand, and the other strokes the viewer-right real right hand at the same matching location and direction. The viewer-left resting hand is not brushed.

Primary section:
Large RUN THE EXPERIMENT panel with the exact procedural steps. This is the most important text block on the poster.

Observation section:
Place WHAT TO WATCH FOR directly beside or beneath the experiment steps so the student experimenter knows what to notice during and after synchronous stroking.

Debrief section:
Use a clear bullet debrief. This is the second most important content area after the experiment steps.

EXCLUSIONS

No slogan.
No promotional footer strip.
No cartoon brain icon.
No decorative icons in content blocks.
No duplicate mini panels.
No brush-stroke banners.
No worksheet-style heavy boxes.
No labels in the main demonstration image.
No sunglasses, headphones, goggles, blindfold, or VR headset.
No sprawling blanket covering the participant's whole torso or the table.
No middle left hand.
No mirrored rubber hand.

No divider cutting through the visible rubber hand.
```
